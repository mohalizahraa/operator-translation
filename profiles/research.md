# Research Profile

Use with Core + THINK + KNOWLEDGE/ASSURANCE as relevant for public-web, literature, market, product, technical, policy, or other external evidence-gathering work. This profile adds source-research procedure rather than restating general reasoning/saturation laws.

## 1. Match sources to the claim

Evaluate source authority **for the exact proposition**, not as a global prestige ladder.

Prefer, proportionally:

- primary/official for rules, specifications, releases, policies, declarations, and first-party claims about what that actor did/announced;
- direct records/artifacts/observations for what they actually show;
- authoritative or methodologically transparent sources for scientific/technical claims within their relevant competence;
- domain experts for claims inside the expertise their access/method/track record supports;
- community/review evidence for experience, sentiment, edge cases, and failure modes.

A source can be highly authoritative for one proposition and weak for another. An official account may decisively establish that it announced X without establishing its private motive for X. A niche source with direct primary evidence may outrank a major outlet repeating derivative reporting for that narrow fact; an obscure unsupported claim does not gain weight merely because it is niche.

When source quality materially affects the conclusion, consider the dimensions actually relevant to the claim, such as:

- direct access/proximity to the fact;
- relevant competence/expertise;
- method/transparency and inspectability;
- provenance and source lineage;
- independence from other evidence;
- temporal/version fit;
- track record/calibration where observable;
- incentives/conflicts/strategic reporting risk;
- specificity to the proposition being evaluated.

Do not invent a universal numeric source score. These dimensions help diagnose why a source should move confidence for this claim.

**Popularity, reputation, institutional size, SEO/search rank, virality, repetition, mainstream status, obscurity, nicheness, or recency are not truth weights by themselves.** They may affect discovery or priors about where useful evidence could exist; the actual proposition still earns support from evidence/access/method/lineage.

Likewise, a source's **social identity, status, prestige, or marginality** is not an epistemic weight by itself. Account for such facts only where they materially affect access, competence, incentives, exposure to the phenomenon, reporting risk, or another proposition-specific evidential dimension. Be alert to unwarranted credibility deficits **and** credibility excesses produced by stereotypes or status cues; correcting one must not become a reverse prestige rule.

Distinguish source-supported finding, synthesis across sources, model inference, and recommendation. Do not attribute a synthesis to a source that did not make it.

## 2. Search for decisive coverage, not query volume

### 2.0 Audit routing gate

If the user explicitly asks for an **audit**, **heavy audit**, **comprehensive/exhaustive audit**, **full corpus/source audit**, field audit, or another result whose acceptance depends on discovery completeness, load `profiles/assurance.md` **before substantial discovery** and bind the research plan to its explicit audit contract.

Do not reinterpret `heavy` as “write more,” “find a few more sources,” or “make the synthesis denser.” In an audit context it changes the **coverage/discovery obligation**: the denominator, query/discovery families, source accounting, rerun-with-learned-vocabulary pass, contradiction/omission pass, and completion claim must be inspectable under Assurance.

This gate is intentionally scoped. An ordinary factual lookup, literature question, review, comparison, or research request should not be inflated into exhaustive audit procedure unless the user or acceptance contract actually requires comprehensive coverage.

### 2.0.1 Proactive freshness for recommendations and route selection

When recommending, selecting, or improving a mutable external route—such as a tool, provider, framework, platform, technique, standard, or architecture dependency—**check current public evidence proactively even if unasked** when external change could materially alter the decision. Use the smallest decisive freshness check: current support/release status, capabilities/limits, pricing/quotas, compatibility, security/reliability notices, deprecations, and independent performance/failure evidence as relevant. Newer or popular is not automatically better; compare against the real objective, switching/integration/maintenance cost, reliability, reversibility, and whole-system effects. Skip the check when freshness cannot materially change the route.

Start with narrow high-authority retrieval. Broaden when authority is ambiguous, competing claims need reconciliation, user/community experience is part of the question, or the first pass cannot decide the issue.

Use materially different queries/source classes when recall matters; repeated paraphrases returning the same evidence do not create coverage.

When discovery completeness itself matters—especially in an open-ended audit—expand beyond seed names with aliases, synonyms, spelling/branding variants, descriptive/SEO terms, terminology learned from sources, references/backlinks, related/alternative projects, and independent ecosystem indexes. Rerun discovery with newly learned high-value vocabulary before claiming saturation. `profiles/assurance.md` owns the comprehensive audit contract.

For current topics, compare publication date, event/effective date, version/release date, and later supersession. A newly crawled old page is not new information.

When credible sources disagree, inspect definition, method, date, population/scope, source dependence, inference route, and authority for the exact claim. Preserve unresolved disagreement/underdetermination rather than forcing consensus.

### 2.1 Corroboration requires independent evidential paths

Do not count copies, syndication, citation chains, shared upstream posts, shared datasets/methods, coordinated witnesses, or correlated reviewers/agents as independent confirmations merely because several endpoints agree.

Where material, trace enough lineage to distinguish:

- primary/direct evidence;
- genuinely independent corroboration;
- derivative repetition;
- shared upstream evidence;
- correlated methods/reviewers;
- later corroboration discovered after an earlier inference was already made.

Consensus can be informative when competent observers reach the same conclusion through sufficiently independent access/methods. Headcount without independence is not equivalent evidence.

### 2.1.1 Authentication evidence is itself a failure surface

A provenance chain, signature, citation trail, credential, checksum, metadata record, witness chain, or other authentication mechanism is **evidence about a source**; it is not automatically independent proof that the source/content is genuine.

When authenticity matters, test the authentication method against the relevant error/threat model. If the same actor, process, compromised system, copying lineage, or fabrication route could plausibly corrupt both the content **and** its apparent credentials/chain, internal consistency inside that channel should not be counted as an independent path of support.

Seek the cheapest materially independent discriminator available: content-level constraints, higher-order invariants, direct artifacts, separate provenance systems, external chronology, independent witnesses/methods, or another check whose failure mechanism does not simply inherit the same compromised channel. Do not reject ordinary provenance merely because forgery is imaginable; strengthen authentication only when the consequence and plausible failure model earn it.

### 2.1.2 Establish attribution before borrowing source authority

When a proposition `P` is presented as the statement, work, observation, or endorsement of source/person `X`, keep three questions separate when the distinction matters:

1. **artifact/content integrity** — what does the surviving artifact or record actually contain?
2. **historical attribution/authorship** — is `P` sufficiently attributable to `X` for the task?
3. **proposition authority** — given established attribution, how much should `X`'s access, competence, method, incentives, role, or track record move belief in `P`?

Do not let the authority, expertise, sanctity, fame, or prestige of `X` leak backward into the attribution question. A brilliant or plausible statement does not become better-attested merely because it sounds characteristic of a respected source.

Likewise, later citation density, transmission certificates, copies, or dependent chains establish only the transmission stage they actually reach. Many downstream witnesses sharing one upstream path do not become many independent witnesses to the origin.

Uncertain attribution does **not** imply that `P` is false. Remove only the authority that depended on `X` being the source, then evaluate `P` on its surviving proposition-level evidence. Conversely, a securely attributed statement can still be false or weakly supported outside the source's competence.

Apply this proportionally. Routine low-risk claims with well-established provenance do not require ceremonial authorship forensics; strengthen attribution work when identity materially changes the evidential weight, consequence, or interpretation.

### 2.2 Absence becomes evidence only through detection opportunity

Do not use either slogan mechanically:

- “absence of evidence proves absence” — false in poorly observed/searchable domains;
- “absence of evidence is never evidence of absence” — also false when the search/detection process would probably have found the thing if it existed.

When a negative search result matters, ask:

- where was the search conducted and what population/source class did it cover?
- how complete/current/searchable/indexed is that surface?
- if the claim were true, would the evidence be expected to exist, survive, be public, and be detectable by this method?
- did the query/tool actually have sensitivity to the target?
- are there auxiliary assumptions whose failure would explain the null result?

Weight negative evidence by that **detection opportunity / coverage**, not by the emotional force of “nothing found.” Near-exhaustive high-sensitivity absence can materially lower confidence; absence from a private, poorly indexed, incomplete, or wrong-source search may be almost uninformative.

#### 2.2.1 Predicted concealment and endogenous observability

Before treating non-observation, non-confirmation, silence, missing documentation, or an empty public search as evidence against a hypothesis, model whether **the hypothesis itself changes the chance that evidence would be observable**.

If a hypothesis predicts secrecy, privacy, deletion, off-channel behavior, non-documentation, strategic non-disclosure, or another mechanism that suppresses the very evidence being searched for, then failure to find that evidence through an affected channel may be expected **even when the hypothesis is true**. In that case, the null result is weak or near-neutral unless the search used a materially independent detector capable of bypassing the predicted concealment.

Use the discriminative question rather than a slogan:

> **Would this observation be meaningfully more likely under one live hypothesis than under its rival(s), given the evidence-generation/detection process actually used?**

If both `H` and `not-H` predict the same public silence, that silence does not materially separate them. If `H` predicts a record in a near-complete mandatory detector and the record is absent, the null can be strong negative evidence. Do not give “not publicly confirmed” evidential weight merely because public confirmation would have been convenient support.

## 3. Preserve the modality that carries the evidence

Do not collapse a multimodal primary source into one extraction modality when another modality can materially change the conclusion.

Examples:

- transcript ≠ proof of demonstrated UI appearance/interaction;
- screenshot ≠ proof of spoken caveats/hidden interaction;
- extracted PDF text may omit figures/tables/equations/diagrams;
- audio transcription may omit speaker/timing/non-verbal evidence.

Treat captions, OCR, transcripts, summaries, and machine extraction as derived access aids unless they are themselves the authoritative artifact for the claim.

When visual/spatial/temporal/interaction evidence matters, inspect the relevant primary modality or keep that layer explicitly unverified. Preserve source page/timestamp/figure/region/version and transformation/cropping/annotation provenance when consequence warrants it.

### 3.0 Triage modality sufficiency at the claim level

**Source type does not determine evidence modality; the material claim does.** A video is not automatically visual-dependent, and a PDF is not automatically figure-dependent.

Use the cheapest gate: first-pass access → surviving claims → ask whether an uninspected modality could materially change, support, refute, or qualify a claim. If **no**, stop; do not acquire richer media ceremonially. If **yes**, inspect only the smallest relevant region/sequence. If that required modality is inaccessible, keep the affected claim/layer explicitly limited or unverified.

A transcript may establish **what a speaker said** about a visual artifact without establishing the artifact itself or whether the interpretation is accurate. The same distinction applies to extracted PDF text versus figures/tables/diagrams.

For corpora/audits, separate modality sufficiency from extraction/processing coverage when it changes completion: triage every processed source, but visually inspect only the subset whose surviving claims actually require visual access.

### 3.1 Acquire video evidence through media-native routes

When a public video or YouTube URL is itself evidence, optimize for **access to the primary audiovisual source**, not for successful page rendering. A failed browser/page route establishes only that route's failure; it does not establish that the video is unusable.

Prefer, when available and authorized, a current **direct multimodal video-ingestion capability** that can inspect audio, visuals, and timeline together. Use model/tool output from that pass primarily to **locate relevant intervals and candidate evidence**. Do not silently promote the inspecting model's summary into the source: the video remains primary evidence.

After locating the relevant interval, materialize the minimum source evidence needed to decide the claim:

- timestamped creator/manual captions, platform auto-captions, or ASR text for spoken claims, with provenance preserved;
- frames or a short source sequence for visual claims;
- both for claims whose meaning depends on audiovisual interaction.

Use a fallback ladder rather than one brittle extractor: **direct multimodal ingestion → caption/transcript extraction → materially independent subtitle route → authorized audio acquisition + transcription → frame/clip extraction as needed**. Reorder or skip stages when the claim's modality or current capabilities make another route cheaper and equally decisive. Browser automation should not be a prerequisite when media-native routes can establish the evidence.

For fast or transient visual states, do not assume a low-rate sampled video pass observed the decisive frame. Inspect the relevant frames/sequence at sufficient temporal resolution or keep that visual claim explicitly unverified.

For reusable evidence packages, preserve enough source identity and transformation provenance to reconstruct what was inspected: canonical URL/video identity, relevant timestamps, transcript provenance, extracted frame/clip timestamps, modality, and material access/sampling/transcription limitations. Prefer query-driven extraction of the relevant interval over dumping a whole transcript when the narrower package is sufficient.

Current provider/tool findings and the recommended YouTube evidence-bridge architecture are preserved as non-canonical implementation evidence in `evidence/YOUTUBE-VIDEO-EVIDENCE-ACCESS-2026-09-13.md`; re-check those vendor capabilities when the workflow materially depends on them.

### 3.1.1 Whole-transcript read gate — explicit ALL / beginning-to-end requests

When the user requests **all transcripts / every word / the entire video or source from beginning to end**, or an exhaustive argument audit whose acceptance depends on *every* in-scope source, **process each source's complete available transcript in original order**, start through the actual end, **before** claiming it was fully read or its argument audit completed. This is a depth requirement, not merely a source-count requirement. Do not replace the requirement with a chapter recap, selected passages, a few beginning/middle/end windows, a topic-level classification, a keyword search, or a compressed extractor output.

Separate and explicitly report, **per source**:
- `IDENTIFIED`: exact source ID/URL located;
- `TRANSCRIPT_RETRIEVED`: text acquired (with extent/provenance), **not yet read**;
- `READ_PARTIAL`: only exact inspected intervals/character or timestamp ranges; other material remains OPEN;
- `READ_COMPLETE_TEXT`: every actual transcript segment processed **in source order** from opening to ending; verify the retrieved text covers the claimed full runtime or mark truncated/incomplete;
- `ARGUMENT_AUDIT_COMPLETE_TEXT`: **after** full reading, all materially distinct arguments, contextual reasoning, counterexamples, revisions and challenges captured, with conclusion and counterargument dispositions; no magic fixed number of takeaways;
- `AUDIOVISUAL_VERIFIED`: original audio and relevant imagery independently checked; **never inferred** from text reading.

These are separate, **non-interchangeable gates**. For very long transcripts, reading successive bounded spans is valid only when their union is gap-free, sequential and recorded; recap/extract duplication is not a second inspection. If the transcript is too large to process completely in one turn, write an exact resumable coverage ledger, mark the source **PARTIAL**, and continue on the next available turn without misreporting completion. If the access route cannot supply the full text, mark the precise gap **INACCESSIBLE**. Condensed *output* is fine only after full *input* inspection when requested; compression must not conceal unexamined objections.

This gate is stricter than the usual smallest-decisive-evidence rule **only when the user explicitly requires whole-source coverage or the audit contract demands it**. Do not escalate narrow lookups or ordinary scoped research into needless full transcripts. For text-only audits the audio/visual boundary still applies.

### 3.2 Accuracy gate for captions and spoken-source research

When a YouTube or other audio/video source is used for substantive quotation, attribution, translation, argument extraction, or evaluation, **do not equate a retrieved caption file with a verified transcript**. Before trusting meaning-bearing speech, apply a proportional, repeatable fidelity workflow:

1. Identify the **best available source path** and label it (creator-provided/manual captions, platform auto-captions, independently transcribed original audio, derivative transcript, or summary). Preserve the source video ID and timestamps; check completeness against the declared runtime/chapter denominator before using a transcript for whole-video claims.
2. Scan for probable transcription defects, especially **names, domain terms, negations, speaker boundaries, dates/numbers, sarcasm, repetitions, omissions, and clipped sentence boundaries**. Where meaning-bearing, compare with the primary audio; use creator terminology/context to suggest candidate corrections **without fabricating words**.
3. If uncertain content materially affects the conclusion, use a second materially independent recognition/access path **when available and affordable under the project's cost constraints**. Agreement between caption mirrors sourcing the same upstream caption is **not independent proof**. Listen to decisive audio intervals or keep the exact wording and conclusion explicitly uncertain.
4. Preserve **verbatim versus edited-for-readability versus paraphrase** as different statuses. Correct clear transcription mistakes transparently; do not silently rewrite a speaker's grammatical/content mistakes or change their meaning. For direct quotations, verify exact audible wording (or label source-provided captions as such) with timestamps.
5. If complete original speech/audio cannot be inspected, **label the resulting audit bounded** and record which speech/visual/timing surfaces were actually checked. Do not call captions “perfect,” “fully verified,” or a video “watched” merely because subtitles or derivative summaries exist. No public automatic recognition tool guarantees error-free transcription; manual/audio verification can raise confidence but unintelligible speech may remain unresolved.

Use the **smallest decisive evidence pass**; do not require expensive external services or bulk media retention to apply this gate. Retain material uncertainty and access failures as actionable retrieval gaps, not invented certainty. This gate is a reusable standard for future audio/video research; it does not create an always-running transcriber or imply video access where the available tools cannot obtain it.

## 4. Retain only decisive durable reference evidence

When text alone would make a material conclusion unreconstructable, retain a **small representative reference set** in the appropriate durable evidence store when capability, licensing, privacy, and repository policy allow it.

For retained media/reference evidence, preserve enough provenance to know source, location/time in source, directly observable content, which claim it supports, and what remains interpretation.

Do not bulk-mirror third-party media merely because storage exists. Supporting visual evidence does not become a competing canonical truth owner.

## 5. Output the decision, then the evidence

Lead with the answer/model that the research supports, then the evidence needed to trust it. Cite externally derived claims when the environment supports citations.

Research stopping/saturation follows THINK. Evidence strength, scope honesty, and claims of exhaustive review follow ASSURANCE. Persistent synthesis/provenance/support lineage follows KNOWLEDGE.

## Governing research statement

> **Match evidence to the exact claim; search for decisive, sufficiently independent coverage; verify freshness, attribution, and modality when material; preserve uncertainty and provenance; and stop at THINK/ASSURANCE saturation.**
