The Prophet’s Standing in the Noble Qurʾan

The Noble Qurʾan establishes proximity, distinction, and lofty standing with Allah, Glorified and Most High, for certain servants. Among them is the Prophet of Allah Moses ﵊. Allah Most High said: ﴾O you who believe, do not be like those who harmed Moses, whereupon Allah cleared him of what they said, and he was distinguished in the sight of Allah.﴿ (al-Aḥzāb 33:69). That is, he possessed standing and rank with Allah because of his obedience to Him.

Among them is the Prophet of Allah Jesus ﵊. Allah Most High said: ﴾When the angels said, “O Mary, Allah gives you glad tidings of a Word from Him, whose name is the Messiah, Jesus son of Mary, distinguished in this world and the Hereafter and among those brought near.”﴿ (Āl ʿImrān 3:45). That is, he possesses standing with Allah, Glorified and Most High, in this world and is among those brought near, and he possesses standing with Him in the Hereafter and is among those brought near. ⟦F200-1⟧

If Jesus and Moses ﵊ possess standing with Allah, Glorified and Most High, and are among those brought near, then Muḥammad ﷺ, the Seal of the Prophets and Messengers, possesses still greater standing and is nearer to Allah than both of them.

Positions on Tawassul through the Prophet’s Standing ﷺ

If the Prophet ﷺ possesses standing with Allah and is among those brought near, and if he has a right with Allah, Glorified and Most High, is it permissible to seek Allah through his standing and right? The First Position: That of the Generality of Muslim Scholars

Muslim scholars of the earlier and later generations—Companions, Successors, earlier authorities, and contemporary scholars—held that tawassul through the Prophet’s standing and right is permissible and commendable. Its permissibility and commendability are matters known to every religious person, and it is the well-known practice of Allah’s righteous servants, the scholars, and the Muslim public. No adherent of the religious traditions was known to have denied this, nor was such a denial heard in any age, until Ibn Taymiyyah came and spoke about it in a manner that confused the weak, innovating a position for which he had no predecessor in earlier ages.

Ibn Ḥajar al-Haytamī said: “Among Ibn Taymiyyah’s absurdities, which no scholar before him had stated and by which he became notorious among the Muslims, is that he denied seeking aid and tawassul.” ⟦F201-1⟧

Al-Subkī said: “It is enough for you that Ibn Taymiyyah’s denial of seeking aid and tawassul is a view no scholar before him had expressed, and by it he became notorious among the Muslims!” ⟦F201-2⟧

Al-ʿAllāmah al-Ālūsī said: “Some people have prohibited tawassul through a person himself and adjuring Allah Most High by any of His creatures absolutely. This is the position toward which the words of the renewer Ibn Taymiyyah incline.” ⟦F201-3⟧

Shaykh ʿAbd Allāh ibn Muḥammad ibn al-Ṣiddīq al-Ghumārī said: “Tawassul, seeking aid, and seeking intercession through the master of humankind, our Prophet Muḥammad ﷺ, the lamp of darkness, are among the emphatically recommended acts, especially in times of grave distress. This was the path followed by the practicing scholars, the worshipping saints, the eminent hadith scholars, and the earlier imams.” ⟦F201-4⟧
