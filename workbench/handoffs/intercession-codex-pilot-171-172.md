The Legitimacy of Seeking Intercession through the Prophet’s Supplication after His Death ....

The transmitted reports also support the view that the prophets and messengers are the witnesses. Al-‘Ayyashi narrated from the Commander of the Faithful (peace be upon him) that he said, describing the Day of Resurrection:

“They will be gathered in a place where all creation will be made to speak. No one will speak except one to whom the All-Merciful has granted permission and who says what is right. Then the messengers will be brought forward and questioned. This is what He says to Muhammad (peace be upon him): ‘So how will it be when We bring from every nation a witness and bring you as a witness over these?’ He is the witness over the witnesses, and the witnesses are the messengers (peace be upon them).”

In the Tafsir of Abu Hatim:

“Abu Sa‘id al-Ashja‘i; Abu ‘Abd al-Rahman al-Harithī told us, from Juwair, from al-Dahhak, regarding ‘So how will it be when We bring from every nation a witness?’ He said: ‘Every nation with its prophet.’” (2)

In Tafsir al-Baghawi:

“His Exalted saying, ‘So how will it be when We bring from every nation a witness?’—that is, how will the situation be, and what will they do, when We bring from every nation a witness, meaning its prophet, to testify against it concerning what they did?” (3)

The Second Category: The Testimony of Those Other Than the Prophets

This, too, is a special testimony, meaning that one testifies concerning the deeds and intentions of a community of people, as in the preceding verse (the eighty-ninth verse of Surat al-Nahl), and His Exalted saying: “And thus We have made you a middle community, so that you may be witnesses over the people and the Messenger may be a witness over you” (al-Baqarah 2:143).

… Seeking Intercession through the Possessor of the Means

The middle community is not the entirety of the community of the Seal of the Prophets, may God bless him and his family and grant them peace; otherwise, the meaning of the verse would be: “And thus We have made you a middle community, so that you may be witnesses over the middle community”—which, as you can see, is untenable. Rather, it is only a part of it, meaning that some testify concerning all, while the Messenger testifies concerning them. Thus, the verse points to three parties:

First: the people, namely, the community of the Seal of the Prophets, may God bless him and his family and grant them peace; they are only those against whom testimony is given.

Second: the middle community, which is part of the community of the Seal; they testify against it, and the Messenger, may God bless him and his family and grant them peace, testifies against them.

Third: the Messenger, may God bless him and his family and grant them peace, who is a witness over the witnesses.

“The statements of the Shi‘i and Sunni exegetes agree that every prophet is a witness over his community. Likewise, in every group and every generation, there must be a witness over them. The witness in the age of the Prophetic mission was the Prophet, may God bless him and his family and grant them peace; and in every age after the time of the Messenger of God, may God bless him and his family and grant them peace, there is a witness who is not a prophet.” (1)

Fakhr al-Razi stated:

“It has also been established that in every age after the time of the Messenger there must be a witness. It follows that no age can be without a witness over the people, and that witness must be one for whom error is not possible; otherwise, he would require another witness, and this would continue without end, which is impossible.” (2)

But after that he identifies the witness as the consensus of the community (3). We agree with him in the argument he presents, but disagree with him regarding the referent he proposes (4).

(1) The Hereafter: A Qur’anic Vision, cited source above, vol. 2, p. 16.

(2) The Great Exegesis, cited source above, vol. 20, p. 99.

(3) Ibid.

(4) That is, the School of the Ahl al-Bayt adopts the theory—“No age or era is without a witness over the community”—advanced by Fakhr al-Razi, but differs from him in identifying the referent.
